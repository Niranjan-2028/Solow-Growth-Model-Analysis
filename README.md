# Solow Growth Model Analysis: China, Japan, South Korea & India

An empirical and theoretical analysis of economic growth, capital accumulation, and productivity across four major Asian economies using the **Solow-Swan Growth Model**.

---

## 📊 Overview & Key Findings

This repository investigates the relationship between capital per worker ($k$), output per worker ($y$), investment, and steady-state dynamics across **China, Japan, South Korea, and India**.

* **China & South Korea:** Show strong evidence of capital deepening with substantial increases in output per worker.
* **Japan:** Demonstrates moderate growth in output per worker alongside a declining capital-per-worker trend, pointing to structural shifts in capital productivity.
* **India:** Features steady growth in capital and output per worker, alongside notable fluctuations in estimated steady-state capital requiring further parametric examination.

### Japan

<img width="4353" height="5025" alt="japan" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e99ab51b-792c-400e-9491-c790df50e1e4" />

Japan presents a distinct structural trajectory compared to China and South Korea.

* **Key Trends:** Output per worker shows moderate growth over time, while capital per worker follows a overall declining trend. Both the investment-per-worker and depreciation series display notable fluctuations.
* **Economic Insight:** The divergence between steady output growth and declining capital per worker suggests a transition toward mature steady-state dynamics, shifting the growth focus toward Total Factor Productivity (TFP) rather than purely capital accumulation.

### South Korea

<img width="4371" height="5026" alt="south_korea" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/a4124302-5bba-4825-920c-7f25c3e8f8a4" />

South Korea demonstrates significant economic transformation characterized by high capital deepening and labor productivity gains.

* **Key Trends:** Both output per worker and capital per worker show upward trajectories over the period analyzed. However, investment rates and estimated steady-state capital display periodic volatility.
* **Economic Insight:** The simultaneous rise in output and capital per worker aligns strongly with capital-intensive growth. Further empirical decomposition is required to evaluate the precise proportion of output growth driven by capital accumulation versus technological progress ($A$).

### India

<img width="4114" height="5228" alt="india" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/49714487-b3b2-4052-a543-110ca7302061" />

India illustrates an emerging economy profile with steady growth in capital accumulation and output capacity.

* **Key Trends:** Output per worker and capital per worker exhibit consistent upward growth. Investment per worker trends steadily upward alongside expanding depreciation needs, while estimated steady-state capital demonstrates noticeable variability across specific years.
* **Economic Insight:** The rising capital-per-worker trend confirms ongoing capital deepening. The observed fluctuations in steady-state capital estimates suggest the need for closer parameter calibration (e.g., depreciation rate $\delta$, population growth rate $n$, and savings rate $s$).
